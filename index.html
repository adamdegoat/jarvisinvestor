◇ What's driving markets
Big Tech is carrying everythingTechnology is up ~38% over three months and is the single reason the index is rising. The AI-spending story keeps drawing institutional money. It's powerful — but it means if Tech wobbles, the whole market feels it.
Oil just rolled over hardCrude oil fell ~14% in a month. That eases inflation pressure and helps consumers (cheaper gas), but it's a headwind for Energy stocks — which are the weakest sector right now.
Rates and the dollar are steady-to-easyThe 10-year Treasury yield is ~4.45% and the US dollar is soft (~99). Neither is tightening the screws on stocks, which keeps the backdrop supportive for risk assets.

The backdrop is supportive: rates are stable (~4.45% on the 10-year), the dollar is soft, and volatility is low. That's a 'goldilocks-ish' setup for stocks — not too hot, not too cold. The one thing to watch isn't a macro number, it's market *internals*: leadership is narrow (Tech-only). The bullish case strengthens a lot if other sectors start joining the rally; it weakens if Tech alone is holding up the index.
🟢 If you're newer to investing: The market is in a calm uptrend, but it's being driven almost entirely by big technology companies. That's good news — until it isn't. Because so much depends on tech right now, a tech stumble would hurt more than usual. The boring-but-smart move: stay invested in the trend, but don't put all your eggs in the hottest names, and don't chase a stock just because it's up a lot. Diversification matters most exactly when one corner of the market is doing all the work.
